## About Me

My name is Bronson Whitmore, and I am pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Data Science with the Economics option at Oregon State University. I expect to graduate in June 2028. I am interested in using data to understand geopolitics, business and technology adoption, and test evidence that supports a claim.

Before returning to school, I served in the U.S. Army in satellite communications, where I led a team of 12 soldiers and delivered technical briefings. I also managed a referral program at Lido DAO. These experiences gave me practical experience in handling dangerous equipment, leading teams and taking care of their families, handling sensitive information, managing irreversible payment processes, and building programs in ambiguous or unverifiable domains.

My coursework has included Python and pandas, along with statistical analysis in R and RStudio. I have worked with descriptive statistics, data visualization, correlation, and regression. I am currently learning more about statistical methods and calculus.

I live in Albany, Oregon, with my family. Outside of school, I enjoy soccer and West Coast Swing dancing.

## Data Science Career Goals

A career direction I would like to pursue using data science is for geopolitical and national security analysis. I enjoy assessing and determining countries’ capabilities and how they use their political systems, geography, institutions, populations, and technologies to pursue national goals. Examining how two adversaries contend with their advantages and constraints is deeply fascinating to me.

Through my Data Science degree and Economics option, I want to develop the tools to investigate broader strategic questions. I am interested in how economic resources and technological development translate into national power, and why countries with similar resources sometimes achieve different results.

Before graduating, I want to strengthen my Python, R, and statistical skills while developing a deeper understanding of international affairs. One possible portfolio project would use public trade or population data to examine a specific national strength or vulnerability. I would explain the data’s limitations and consider competing explanations for the patterns I find. I would also like to explore an internship or research opportunity that combines data analysis with international affairs. My longer-term goal is to produce assessments that distinguish a country’s capabilities from its intentions, make assumptions clear, and communicate uncertainty.
